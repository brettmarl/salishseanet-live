Host a Lookout.

Seanet Lookouts live on decks, docks, rooftops and boathouses around the Salish Sea. Each one fills in another piece of the picture of what's moving on the water. Here's what it takes to host one.

What a host provides

  • A spot for a small box, and an antenna mounted as high as practical
  • A power outlet or a Power-over-Ethernet port
  • Internet access over Wi-Fi (wired Ethernet coming soon)

What we provide

  • The Seanet Lookout itself: the box, antenna and mounting hardware
  • Setup and registration with the AIS data networks
  • Remote monitoring and software updates
  • The Salish Seanet app, to see the boats your Lookout hears

What makes a great site

  • A clear view over open water
  • Height: radio travels by line of sight, so every extra foot lets your Lookout hear farther
  • Nothing tall in the way: hills, trees and buildings block the signal

Exclusive to hosts

See what your Lookout hears.

Your Lookout is listening to the boats around you. The Salish Seanet app lets you listen in, too. See every boat your Lookout picks up, follow them on a live map, or point your phone across the water to find out exactly what you're looking at.

The view from your shore, with names attached.

Wondering about that ferry rounding the point? The tug working its way up the channel? The ship passing in the distance?

Your Lookout is already tracking them. The app puts that information in your hands. Browse the boats your Lookout hears, see where they're moving on a map, and tap for photos and details. Or switch to Look mode and see their names floating over the water, right where the boats are.

Power, internet and safety

Power: about $3 a year

About two watts, less than a single LED light bulb. That's roughly $3 a year at OPALCO rates.

Internet: you won't notice it

No effect on your download speed. It uses a tiny bit of upload, spread through the day. Over a whole day, that's about the same as loading a few web pages.

Safe on your network

It only sends AIS data out. It accepts no incoming connections and never talks to your other devices.
