For station hosts

Thank you for hosting.

Seanet Lookouts live on decks, docks, rooftops and boathouses around the Salish Sea. Hosts make the network possible. Here's what's involved, and what to do if something looks wrong.

What a host provides

  • A spot for a small box, and an antenna mounted as high as practical
  • A power outlet or a Power-over-Ethernet port
  • Internet access over Wi-Fi (wired Ethernet coming soon)

What we provide

  • The Seanet Lookout itself: the box, antenna and mounting hardware
  • Setup and registration with the AIS data networks
  • Remote monitoring and software updates

A great site

A clear, unobstructed view over open water. Height matters most: VHF radio travels by line of sight, so every bit of extra antenna height extends how far the station can hear.

Power, internet and safety

Power: about $3 a year

About two watts, less than a single LED light bulb. That's roughly $3 a year at OPALCO rates.

Internet: you won't notice it

No effect on your download speed. It uses a tiny bit of upload, spread through the day. Over a whole day, that's about the same as loading a few web pages.

Safe on your network

It only sends AIS data out. It accepts no incoming connections and never talks to your other devices.

If something looks wrong

We monitor every station and usually notice a problem before you do, but if you think yours has stopped working:

  1. Restart it. Unplug the power (or network cable, for Power over Ethernet), wait ten seconds, and plug it back in. It's ready again within a minute.
  2. Changed your Wi-Fi? If you have a new router, network name or password, the station needs the new details. Get in touch and we'll walk you through it. It takes a couple of minutes with our setup app, during the first 30 seconds after the station powers up.
  3. Moving or retiring a station? Let us know before you take it down, so we can update the data networks.

The station is safe to unplug at any time.
